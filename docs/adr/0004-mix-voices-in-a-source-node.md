# Mix voices in a source node, not a pool of player nodes

The spec called for a pool of `AVAudioPlayerNode`s that stay playing, with buffers scheduled into them, one voice count per binding, retrigger by stealing the single voice, a peak limiter, and a small random pitch and gain per hit. Two of those cannot hold at once. Retrigger means cutting a sound mid-buffer on the next hit, and a hard cut steps the waveform, which is an audible click; a player node has no way to fade an arbitrary cut. Per-hit pitch is also awkward there, since it needs a varispeed unit per voice, and the limiter would sit on the mix regardless. So the mechanism in the spec conflicts with the spec's own "no audible clicks, pops, or clipping" requirement.

We render the mix ourselves in one `AVAudioSourceNode` instead. Every hit is a voice with its own read cursor, pitch, and gain. Starting or stealing a voice ramps it in or out over a few milliseconds, so nothing ever steps, and a feed-forward limiter caps the sum at just under full scale. The binding's voice count bounds how many copies hold full level; voices already fading out do not count. The cost is that the app owns the sample loop and sample-rate conversion, a few dozen lines that run at trivial cost for a handful of sounds.

Considered and rejected: pre-fading each decoded buffer and retriggering with `scheduleBuffer(.interrupts)`. The fade fixes a buffer's own endpoints but not the mid-buffer cut that retrigger makes, so the click survives.
