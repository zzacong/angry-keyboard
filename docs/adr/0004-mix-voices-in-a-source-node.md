# Render the keystroke mix in one source node, and trim silence at load

The spec called for a pool of `AVAudioPlayerNode`s that stay playing, with buffers scheduled into them, retrigger by stealing the single voice, a peak limiter, and a small random pitch and gain per hit. Two of those cannot hold at once. Retrigger cuts a sound mid-buffer on the next hit, and a hard cut steps the waveform into an audible click; a player node has no way to fade an arbitrary cut. Per-hit pitch is also awkward there, since it needs a varispeed unit per voice. The mechanism conflicts with the spec's own "no audible clicks, pops, or clipping" requirement.

We render the mix ourselves in one `AVAudioSourceNode` instead. Every hit is a voice, and starting or stealing one ramps it in or out over a few milliseconds, so nothing ever steps. A limiter caps the summed voices at just under full scale. Voices are counted per pool, capped by the triggering binding's voice count, and a voice already fading out does not count. The cost is that the app owns the sample loop and sample-rate conversion, a few dozen lines that run at trivial cost for a handful of sounds.

Some supplied sounds carry dead air at the head, up to about 300 ms, so untrimmed keys feel late and a held key produces no row at all: every key repeat restarts the file inside its own silence and the sound never emerges. We trim at load, in memory, unconditionally, dropping everything before the first audible window and applying the usual end fades to the new start. The trim is a level heuristic, not a content detector, so a deliberately quiet lead-in would lose it. That favors an immediate answer over preserving an authored lead-in, and the bundled files stay untouched with no per-file configuration.

Considered and rejected: pre-fading each decoded buffer and retriggering with `scheduleBuffer(.interrupts)`. The fade fixes a buffer's own endpoints but not the mid-buffer cut that retrigger makes, so the click survives. Considered and rejected: a per-file trim offset in the pack. It puts a second, easily stale copy of each sound's shape in the model, and a new sound would need someone to measure it by hand. Considered and rejected: trimming the tail too. A sound's decay is part of how it reads.
